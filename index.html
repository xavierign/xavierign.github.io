What you’ll see

“FaCells” are portraits decomposed into 15 facial attributes—arched eyebrows, bangs, eyeglasses, and more. On site, a plotter performs like a second pair of hands for the model, translating what the network “thinks” it knows into physical lines on paper.

How the model works

Unlike typical image/text systems, our neural network is trained purely on lines. Each attribute becomes a cell in the network. It’s intentionally an imperfect learner: it reflects human labeling biases (e.g., notions like “attractive”) and reads no color—only patterns of stroke and contour. The point isn’t perfection; it’s to probe what “understanding” means when a machine learns our categories.

Why it matters

By letting the machine draw, we expose both the elegance and the limits of data‑driven perception. Every portrait is a conversation starter about how culture, bias, and tooling shape what machines learn.
